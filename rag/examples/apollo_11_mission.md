# Apollo 11 Lunar Landing Mission Overview

## Mission Background
Apollo 11 was the American spaceflight that first landed humans on the Moon on July 20, 1969. Launched by a Saturn V rocket from Kennedy Space Center on Merritt Island, Florida, on July 16, it was the fifth crewed mission of NASA's Apollo program.

## Crew Members
- **Commander:** Neil Armstrong
- **Lunar Module Pilot:** Edwin "Buzz" Aldrin
- **Command Module Pilot:** Michael Collins

## Lunar Landing & Surface Operations
Commander Neil Armstrong and Lunar Module Pilot Buzz Aldrin landed the Apollo Lunar Module *Eagle* at Mare Tranquillitatis (the Sea of Tranquility) on July 20, 1969, at 20:17 UTC. Armstrong radioed to Houston: "Houston, Tranquility Base here. The Eagle has landed."

Six hours and 39 minutes later on July 21 at 02:56 UTC, Armstrong stepped onto the lunar surface and stated: "That's one small step for man, one giant leap for mankind." Aldrin joined him 19 minutes later.

While Armstrong and Aldrin were on the lunar surface, Michael Collins flew the Command Module *Columbia* alone in lunar orbit. The two astronauts spent a total of 21 hours and 36 minutes on the Moon's surface, during which they collected 47.5 pounds (21.5 kg) of lunar rocks and soil samples before lifting off to rendezvous with Collins in orbit.
