# Quantum Computing Fundamentals & Qubits

## 1. Classical vs. Quantum Computing
Classical computers process information using binary electrical signals known as bits, which strictly occupy either a state of 0 or a state of 1. Quantum computers replace binary bits with quantum bits, or qubits, harnessing quantum mechanics principles to perform specific complex calculations exponentially faster than classical supercomputers.

## 2. Core Quantum Principles
- **Superposition:** A qubit can exist in a linear combination of state |0⟩ and state |1⟩ simultaneously. It only collapses into a deterministic 0 or 1 state upon physical measurement.
- **Entanglement:** When multiple qubits become entangled, the quantum state of each qubit cannot be described independently of the others. Measuring one entangled qubit instantaneously determines the state of its paired qubit, regardless of distance.
- **Quantum Decoherence:** Qubits are fragile and susceptible to thermal noise and electromagnetic radiation, causing decoherence and loss of quantum information. Modern quantum systems require cryogenic cooling to near absolute zero (approximately 15 millikelvins).

## 3. High-Impact Applications
- **Molecular Simulation:** Simulating molecular catalysts for battery chemistry and pharmaceutical drug discovery.
- **Cryptography:** Shor's algorithm provides polynomial-time integer factorization, necessitating Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) standards.
- **Optimization:** Solving combinatorial logistics and financial portfolio optimization problems using quantum annealing and variational quantum eigensolvers (VQE).
